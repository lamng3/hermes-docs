    Planner

    A query becomes a relational-algebra plan, the optimizer rewrites it with the graph's statistics, and the plan executor runs it against a graph that answers only single-pattern queries.

    Relational algebra

    A triple pattern is a relation over its variables, a basic graph pattern the natural join of its patterns, and every other SPARQL operator a relational one: σ FILTER, π SELECT, ⟕ OPTIONAL, ∪ UNION, − MINUS, ε BIND, δ DISTINCT, τ ORDER BY, λ LIMIT and OFFSET, γ GROUP BY. translate keeps the patterns in the written order, so every reordering is the optimizer's and can be measured. Source: algebra.py.

    hermes plan --graph corporate --query 'SELECT ?name ?email WHERE { ?p pv:email ?email . ?p pv:name ?name .
  ?p pv:memberOf ?d . ?d pv:name "Marketing" . FILTER(CONTAINS(?name, "S")) }'

    Estimates

    System R (Selinger et al., SIGMOD 1979), from the graph's statistics: per predicate its triples, distinct subjects and objects; per class its instances; property paths from their predicates. A scan joined after operands that bind its variables is priced as an index access, about (distinct bindings) × (rows per binding), because that is how the executor runs it. The cost of a plan is C_out, the summed sizes of its scans and joins. Source: estimate.py.

    Rewrite rules

    Each rule is a command that rewrites the plan and logs what it did (rules.py); the optimizer applies them in order, and a list of rules can be passed instead.

    • push selections: each σ moves to the lowest operator that binds all of its variables in every solution.
    • order joins: each join group is reordered by dynamic programming over left-deep orders without cartesian products, minimizing C_out; each selection goes right after the operand that binds its last variable.
    • prune scans: a scan fetches only the variables used above it.

    Executors

    The plan executor runs scans as single-pattern queries and joins as hash joins, or bind joins that send the left side's bindings with the scan (up to 500, 100 per query); every other operator is evaluated by rdflib's SPARQL engine over its children's rows, with EXISTS and property paths reading the graph through the same single-pattern queries. The engine executor sends the query text to the store and is the reference. Source: executor.py.
